Nobel Prizes of twenty twenty five and twenty twenty six.

Every October a phone rings early in the morning somewhere in the world. A voice from Stockholm or Oslo says, you have won the Nobel Prize. Here are the latest winners, and what they did.

First, twenty twenty five. The Peace Prize went to Maria Corina Machado of Venezuela, for her long and peaceful struggle for democracy.

Physics went to John Clarke, Michel Devoret and John Martinis. They showed that strange quantum effects can happen in an electric circuit big enough to hold in your hand. That work helps build quantum computers.

Chemistry went to Susumu Kitagawa, Richard Robson and Omar Yaghi, for metal organic frameworks. These are crystals full of tiny rooms that can catch water from desert air or trap carbon dioxide.

Medicine went to Mary Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell and Shimon Sakaguchi, who found the cells that stop our immune system from attacking our own body.

Literature went to Laszlo Krasznahorkai of Hungary. Economics went to Joel Mokyr, Philippe Aghion and Peter Howitt, for explaining how new ideas and technology drive growth.

Now twenty twenty six, announced this October. Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel, for optogenetics, a way to switch brain cells on and off with light.

Physics went to Francis Halzen, who led the IceCube observatory, buried deep in the ice of the South Pole. It caught tiny particles called neutrinos, coming from deep space.

Chemistry went to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai, who showed how molecules can come out in only one mirror image form, a puzzle at the heart of life and medicine.

Literature went to the Canadian poet Anne Carson. The Peace Prize went to Navi Pillay of South Africa, a judge and former United Nations human rights chief, for her work for peace and international law. And Economics went to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford, for showing how an economy can be kept steady.

Remember our own story too. Abdus Salam won the Physics Prize in nineteen seventy nine, and Malala Yousafzai won the Peace Prize in twenty fourteen. They are Pakistan's two Nobel laureates.
