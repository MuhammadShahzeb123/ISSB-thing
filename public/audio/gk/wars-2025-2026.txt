The wars of twenty twenty five and twenty twenty six.

Since twenty twenty five, the region around Pakistan has hardly had a quiet month. Wars now come with code names, and examiners love to ask them.

It began on the seventh of May twenty twenty five. After the Pahalgam attack in Kashmir, India struck Pakistan and called it Operation Sindoor. Pakistan hit back on the tenth of May with Operation Bunyan um Marsoos, meaning a wall of lead. The same day a ceasefire was announced. Pakistan thanked the United States for helping, while India said the two armies agreed it directly.

A month later, on the thirteenth of June, Israel attacked Iran in Operation Rising Lion. On the twenty second of June, American bombers hit Iran's nuclear sites in Operation Midnight Hammer. A ceasefire came after twelve days.

Then Pakistan's western border caught fire. In October twenty twenty five, Afghan Taliban forces attacked border posts, and Qatar and Turkey helped arrange a ceasefire. But attacks by militants based in Afghanistan went on. On the night of the twenty sixth of February twenty twenty six, Pakistan launched Operation Ghazab lil Haq, Righteous Fury, against the Taliban regime and its terrorist allies. There was a short pause for Eid in March, but the operation went on, and there is still no lasting ceasefire.

Two days later, on the twenty eighth of February, the United States and Israel struck Iran again. America called it Operation Epic Fury. Iran's Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, was killed, and his son Mojtaba Khamenei became the third Supreme Leader. After thirty eight days, a ceasefire began in April. It broke down in July, when Iran attacked ships in the Strait of Hormuz and America struck back. In October the two sides are still talking, with no final deal.

Older wars go on too. Russia and Ukraine have fought since twenty twenty two. In Sudan, the army fights the Rapid Support Forces. Israel's war on Gaza began in October twenty twenty three. Myanmar, Yemen and eastern Congo are still at war.

Why does this matter for Pakistan? Oil prices, trade routes and our two long borders all depend on these fights. A future officer must know who fought whom, when, and where it stands now.
