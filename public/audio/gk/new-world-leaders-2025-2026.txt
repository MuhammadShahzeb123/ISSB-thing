New faces in power.

Leaders change, and the old answer in your notebook can become wrong overnight. Since January twenty twenty five, many countries have new faces at the top.

Start with the big powers. Donald Trump returned to the White House in January twenty twenty five as the forty seventh President of the United States. He had also been the forty fifth. In Canada, Mark Carney became Prime Minister in March. In Germany, Friedrich Merz became Chancellor in May.

In Asia, Lee Jae myung became President of South Korea in June, after his predecessor was removed. In Japan, Sanae Takaichi became the first woman Prime Minister in October.

Europe kept changing. Karol Nawrocki became President of Poland in August. In France, Sebastien Lecornu was named Prime Minister in September, and named again in October. In the Czech Republic, Andrej Babis returned as Prime Minister in December.

In Africa, Netumbo Nandi Ndaitwah became the first woman President of Namibia. In Madagascar, protests forced out the President, and Colonel Michael Randrianirina of the army was sworn in that October.

Latin America moved too. Rodrigo Paz became President of Bolivia in November twenty twenty five. Jose Antonio Kast took office in Chile in March twenty twenty six.

Now our own neighbourhood. In Bangladesh, the BNP won a landslide election, and Tarique Rahman became Prime Minister in February twenty twenty six. In Nepal, after the youth protests, the former rapper and Kathmandu mayor Balendra Shah, known as Balen, became Prime Minister in March, at just thirty five. And in Iran, after Ali Khamenei was killed in the war, his son Mojtaba Khamenei became Supreme Leader.

Why learn these names? Pakistan deals with every one of these governments. When the panel asks who leads Bangladesh or Iran today, the right answer shows that you read the news, not just old books.
