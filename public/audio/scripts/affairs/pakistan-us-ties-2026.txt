Pakistan and America grow close again.

On 22 September 2026, Donald Trump spoke to world leaders at the United Nations in New York. He called Pakistan's prime minister, Shehbaz Sharif, a "terrific man".

For years, Pakistan and US ties went up and down. The change came in 2025, around the short war with India in May. Trump said he helped stop it, while India denies any outside role.

On 18 June 2025, Trump invited army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir to lunch at the White House. Reuters said no US president had hosted Pakistan's army chief there before without senior civilian officials.

Business followed. On 31 July 2025, both countries agreed a trade deal. The US tariff, a tax on imported goods, on Pakistani products fell from a threatened 29 percent to 19 percent. India got 25 percent.

Minerals became the new hope. On 8 September 2025, the American firm US Strategic Metals signed a deal worth about 500 million dollars with the Frontier Works Organisation. In December 2025, a US government bank approved 1.25 billion dollars for the Reko Diq copper and gold mine.

On 25 September 2025, Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Munir met Trump at the White House. They showed him a wooden box of minerals.

Then came the biggest test. On 28 February 2026, the US and Israel went to war with Iran, Pakistan's neighbour. On 7 April 2026, Trump agreed to a 2-week ceasefire. He said it was based on conversations with Shehbaz Sharif and Asim Munir.

On 11 and 12 April 2026, Islamabad hosted face-to-face talks between the US and Iran. Vice President JD Vance led the American team. The talks lasted 21 hours but ended without a deal. On 17 June 2026, both presidents signed the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, a 14-point plan for peace. Shehbaz Sharif signed it as mediator, or go-between.

In early July 2026, the top US diplomat in Pakistan, Natalie Baker, said ties were "stronger than it's ever been". Soon after, the Iran deal broke down and fighting returned. Pakistan kept urging both sides to return to it.

Why should ordinary Pakistanis care? America is the biggest single buyer of Pakistani goods, mostly textiles. Pakistan sold about 6 billion dollars of goods there in the year to June 2025. A lower tariff helps protect factory jobs at home.

Yet tariffs keep changing. On 20 February 2026, the US Supreme Court struck down Trump's emergency tariffs, including the 19 percent rate. Since 24 July 2026, most Pakistani goods face a new 10 percent tariff. A full trade agreement is still being negotiated.

Pakistan also still needs money. In July 2026, it asked Washington for a 10 billion dollar facility, a backup fund, to support the rupee.

There was friction too. On 19 August 2026, the US ambassador to India called Indian-held Kashmir "an important part of India". Pakistan protested strongly. US officials later said their position had not changed.

The lesson is practical. Warm words at the top help, but they can change fast. Pakistan should turn goodwill into signed agreements, working mines and real jobs, while keeping its friendship with China strong.

Good ties with Washington are worth having. They are worth even more when they do not depend on a single phone call.
