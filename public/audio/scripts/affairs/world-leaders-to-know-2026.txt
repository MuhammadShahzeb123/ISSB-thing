World leaders to know in 2026.

In 2026 the world changed faster than usual. New faces took power in Tehran, London and Dhaka. Here are the leaders who matter most to Pakistan, sorted by one simple question. Why does this person matter to us?

First come our neighbours. China is our closest friend. President Xi Jinping leads China, and Premier Li Qiang runs its government. In 2026, the 2 countries celebrate 75 years of official ties.

India is a difficult neighbour. Narendra Modi is Prime Minister and Droupadi Murmu is President. S. Jaishankar, the External Affairs Minister, is India's top diplomat. Pakistan and India fought a short conflict in May 2025, and tension between them is still high.

Afghanistan is ruled by the Taliban. Their supreme leader is Hibatullah Akhundzada, and Mohammad Hasan Akhund is acting Prime Minister. Since late February 2026, the 2 countries have clashed along the border. Pakistan says the TTP, a banned militant group, attacks from Afghan soil. The Taliban deny this.

Iran changed suddenly this year. On 28 February 2026, the United States and Israel attacked Iran, and Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed. On 8 March 2026, his son Mojtaba Khamenei became Supreme Leader. Masoud Pezeshkian is still President.

Pakistan became a mediator, a helper who brings 2 sides to talk. It helped arrange a ceasefire on 8 April 2026. On 17 June 2026, the US and Iran signed the Islamabad Memorandum, a written promise to work for peace. But fighting returned in July.

Second come our Muslim partners. In Saudi Arabia, King Salman is the ruler. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman is also Prime Minister. Türkiye, led by President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, is another close partner.

On 7 August 2026, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye signed the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement. An attack on one will count as an attack on all.

Bangladesh has a new leader too. Tarique Rahman became Prime Minister on 17 February 2026. Before him, Muhammad Yunus led a temporary government as Chief Adviser.

Third come the great powers. In the United States, Donald Trump is President and JD Vance is Vice President. Marco Rubio is Secretary of State, the top American diplomat.

Vladimir Putin leads Russia. In Britain, Andy Burnham became Prime Minister on 20 July 2026, after Keir Starmer resigned.

Emmanuel Macron leads France, and Friedrich Merz is Chancellor of Germany. Sanae Takaichi is Japan's first woman Prime Minister. Ursula von der Leyen heads the European Commission, which runs the daily work of the European Union.

Last come the big organisations. António Guterres is UN Secretary-General until 31 December 2026. The Security Council is choosing the next one, and Pakistan has a vote in that choice.

Khalilur Rahman of Bangladesh became President of the UN General Assembly in September 2026. Ismail Ould Cheikh Ahmed of Mauritania became head of the OIC on 27 August 2026. The OIC is a group of 57 mostly Muslim countries.

Mark Rutte leads NATO, the Western military alliance. Nurlan Yermekbayev leads the SCO, a regional group where Pakistan is a full member and now the chair.

Kristalina Georgieva heads the IMF, which supports Pakistan through a 7 billion dollar loan programme. Ajay Banga leads the World Bank. Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus leads the World Health Organization.

Names can change quickly, as 2026 showed. But the lesson stays the same. Strong friends and calm diplomacy keep Pakistan safe.

Points to remember.
