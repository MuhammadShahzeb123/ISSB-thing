Bangladesh chooses a new leader after the uprising.

On 17 February 2026, Tarique Rahman stood outside parliament in Dhaka and took the oath as prime minister.

Less than 2 months before, he was still living in London after 17 years abroad. He flew home on 25 December 2025, and his mother, former prime minister Khaleda Zia, died 5 days later.

To understand this moment, go back to July 2024. Students rose up against Sheikh Hasina, who had ruled for 15 years. A United Nations human rights team put the death toll at about 1,400.

On 5 August 2024, Hasina fled to India. An interim government, meaning a temporary one, took over under Nobel prize winner Muhammad Yunus.

In May 2025, it banned all activities of her party, the Awami League. The party could not run in the election.

On 17 November 2025, a special court, the International Crimes Tribunal, found Hasina guilty of crimes against humanity over the crackdown. It sentenced her to death in absentia, which means she was not in court. Hasina denies the charges and calls the trial unfair.

The general election came on 12 February 2026. The Bangladesh Nationalist Party, or BNP, and its allies won 212 of the 300 seats. That is more than 2 out of every 3.

Jamaat-e-Islami, the main Islamic party, had its best result ever with 68 seats. The student-led National Citizen Party, born in the 2024 protests, won just 6.

On the same day, a referendum, or direct public vote, approved the July Charter. This is a set of reforms written after the uprising. Among its ideas is a limit of 10 years for any prime minister.

The hardest test is next door. Bangladesh shares a 4,096 km border with India, which wants a fresh start. But Hasina still lives in India, and Dhaka wants her sent back.

On 5 August 2026, Hasina took live questions from journalists in Delhi and promised to return home in December. Dhaka reacted with anger, and Rahman's planned visit to India was put on hold. In September, Bangladesh skipped the BRICS summit in New Delhi.

Rahman is also building other friendships. In June 2026, his first big trips abroad were to Malaysia and China. Ties with Pakistan are warming too, with direct flights and new trade.

Bangladesh's lesson is clear. When rulers stop listening to young people, even a strong leader can fall in weeks. The BNP itself was accused of corruption when it last ruled, so it must earn trust again.

A calm Bangladesh that trades with all its neighbours helps the whole region. Pakistan's friendship with Dhaka should rest on respect and trade, never on Dhaka's problems with Delhi.

Bangladesh has changed its rulers through the ballot box. Now its leaders must prove they can change old habits.

Points to remember.

Sheikh Hasina was sentenced to death in absentia on 17 November 2025. She has lived in India since 5 August 2024.
In the 12 February 2026 election the BNP alliance won 212 of 300 seats. Jamaat won 68, its best ever.
Tarique Rahman, son of Ziaur Rahman and Khaleda Zia, was sworn in as prime minister on 17 February 2026.
A referendum on the same day approved the July Charter of reforms.
Hasina's press event in Delhi on 5 August 2026 stalled Rahman's planned India visit.
