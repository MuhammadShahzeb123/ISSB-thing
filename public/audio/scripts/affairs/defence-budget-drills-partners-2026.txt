Drills, partners and a 3 trillion rupee budget.

On 22 September 2026, Pakistani soldiers began 2 weeks of training with Russian troops at Cherat. On the same day, another group started training with Omani soldiers at the National Counter Terrorism Centre in Pabbi.

The drills, Druzhba-IX and Washaq Al Jabal-II, focused on counter-terrorism skills. They look like small events. But they show how armies stay ready.

An exercise is a practice battle. It lets soldiers test their plans before a real crisis. With foreign partners, it also builds interoperability, which means forces can talk, plan and work together smoothly.

Pakistan's training calendar has been busy. From 7 to 11 February 2025, the navy hosted AMAN-25 off Karachi, with about 60 countries taking part. From 28 November to 14 December 2025, the army trained with China's army in Warrior-IX.

In January 2026, air force F-16s flew non-stop to Saudi Arabia for Spears of Victory 2026. Air forces from the US, the UK, France and others joined too.

In February 2026, 19 countries sent teams to the Pakistan Army Team Spirit competition at Kharian. They included the US and Saudi Arabia.

The navies of Pakistan and China held Sea Guardian IV from 25 March 2026. In April, Pakistani commandos trained in Türkiye in Jinnah-XIII. In September, Pakistani special forces joined Türkiye, Azerbaijan and Uzbekistan for Eternal Brotherhood-5.

Some partnerships now go beyond training. On 17 September 2025, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed a Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement. It says aggression against either country is aggression against both.

In 2026, war in the region brought missile and drone attacks on Gulf states. On 11 April 2026, Saudi Arabia said Pakistani fighter jets and support aircraft had arrived at King Abdulaziz Air Base. At the same time, Pakistan was hosting talks in Islamabad aimed at ending the war.

On 7 August 2026, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye signed the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement. An armed attack on any of them will be treated as an attack on all 3.

On 25 September 2026, Field Marshal Asim Munir met the top military officers of Saudi Arabia and Türkiye in Riyadh. They agreed to turn the pact's promises into real military cooperation without delay.

All this costs money. On 12 June 2026, the government proposed 3 trillion rupees for defence services in the new financial year. That is 17.65 percent more than the previous year's original budget of 2.55 trillion rupees.

Dawn reported that this equals about 2.08 percent of GDP, the total value of the economy. Money for weapons and equipment rose by almost 40 percent. Military pensions, budgeted at 822 billion rupees, are counted separately.

Pakistan also serves the wider world. On 29 May 2026, ISPR shared some numbers.

More than 237,000 Pakistani peacekeepers have served on United Nations missions since 1960. Of them, 183 gave their lives. Today they serve in places such as the Democratic Republic of the Congo, South Sudan and the Central African Republic.

The prime minister said Pakistan is the fifth-largest contributor to UN peacekeeping.

A sensible view sees both sides. Every rupee spent on defence is a rupee not spent on schools or hospitals. So the money must buy real readiness, not show.

Training is often the best value of all. Armies that practise together in peace are better prepared, and strong preparation makes war less likely.
