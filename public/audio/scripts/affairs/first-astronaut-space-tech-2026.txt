Pakistan's first astronaut is almost ready to fly.

In a training centre in Beijing, 2 Pakistani pilots spin inside a centrifuge. This machine whirls them around fast to copy the heavy force of a rocket launch.

Their names are Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud. Later in 2026, 1 of them is due to become Pakistan's first astronaut.

The plan became official on 28 February 2025. SUPARCO, Pakistan's space agency, signed an agreement in Islamabad with China's human spaceflight office.

China agreed to train Pakistanis and fly 1 of them to Tiangong, its space station in orbit. It was the first time China would train foreign astronauts.

About 20 finalists were picked from a pool of pilots and scientists. They were tested for health, mental strength and the ability to handle strong g-force.

G-force is the heavy push the body feels during a rocket launch or a sharp turn in a fast jet. That is why pilots used to high-speed flying were chosen.

On 22 April 2026, China named the 2 men as its first foreign astronaut trainees. On 24 April 2026, they entered the Astronaut Center of China in Beijing.

Since then they have been learning Mandarin, the main Chinese language. They have studied the spacecraft and practised eating, sleeping and fixing equipment while weightless.

In a Reuters report on 28 September 2026, a SUPARCO official said the final choice is expected in mid to late October. The chosen pilot will fly from China and spend about 1 week on Tiangong.

His job title will be payload specialist. This means he will run science experiments, not fly the spacecraft. He will also be the first foreigner ever to board Tiangong.

SUPARCO received more than 300 research ideas from universities in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad. Chinese institutes chose 4 biotechnology experiments linked to food security and climate change.

This flight is part of a bigger effort. Between January 2025 and February 2026, Pakistan launched 4 satellites from China that take pictures of the Earth. The latest, EO-2, went up on 12 February 2026.

HS-1, launched on 19 October 2025, can see hundreds of narrow bands of light. This helps experts check the health of crops and water supplies.

These satellites help people on the ground. In 2026, SUPARCO used satellite images to watch 131 glacial lake sites for flood danger. A Pakistani-built rover is also due to fly on China's Chang'e 8 Moon mission around 2028.

Technology is changing life at home too. On 10 March 2026, Pakistan sold 5G spectrum for 507 million dollars. 5G is the newest and fastest type of mobile internet, and spectrum means the radio waves that carry it.

Jazz and Zong started 5G service in major cities on 19 March 2026.

On 30 July 2025, the federal cabinet approved a National Artificial Intelligence Policy. Artificial intelligence means computer systems that learn from data and help make decisions. The policy aims to train 1 million AI professionals by 2030.

The honest view is that Pakistan still depends on China for rockets, spacecraft and training. Real independence will come when our own universities produce the engineers who build such machines.

For now, 2 disciplined pilots carry a whole country's hopes. The flight will last about 1 week, but its lesson about patience, training and teamwork should last much longer.
