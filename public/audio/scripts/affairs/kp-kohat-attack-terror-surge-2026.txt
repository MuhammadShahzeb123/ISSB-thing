Kohat attack and Pakistan's hardest fight against terror.

On 18 September 2026, police officers and local people were at Friday prayers in Kohat, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, or KP. Their mosque stood inside the Old Police Lines, a large police compound. Just after 1 pm, a car full of explosives hit the gate and blew up.

Gunmen then rushed inside. Police officers kept fighting, even while wounded. Investigators later said the attackers wanted to free prisoners held by the Counter Terrorism Department in the same compound.

The battle lasted about 21 hours. At least 23 people were killed, most of them police officers. All 8 attackers died.

A group called Ittehad-ul-Mujahideen Pakistan claimed the attack. It is an alliance of militant groups led by a commander named Hafiz Gul Bahadur. Police said the group works from Afghanistan.

The main threat in KP comes from the TTP, or Pakistani Taliban, and its allies. The state calls the TTP Fitna al-Khawarij. The name says the group twists the true teachings of Islam.

The Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies, or PICSS, is a research group in Islamabad. It counted 3,413 deaths in attacks and security operations in 2025, up from 1,950 in 2024. More than half of the dead were militants.

PICSS says 2025 was also the deadliest year for security forces since 2011. In March 2026, the Global Terrorism Index, a yearly world ranking, named Pakistan the country most hurt by terrorism in 2025. It was the first time this had happened.

2026 looks even worse. In the first 9 months, PICSS recorded 1,232 militant attacks, 52 percent more than in the same months of 2025. September alone saw 225 attacks, the highest monthly total in 12 years.

KP has carried a heavy share. On 16 February 2026, a car bomb at a Bajaur checkpoint killed 11 security personnel and a young girl. On 9 May 2026, attackers destroyed a police post in Bannu and killed at least 15 officers.

On 2 August 2026, a suicide bomber hit a police station in Swat during a peace rally across the road, and 17 people died. A police officer had stopped him at the gate. On 26 September 2026, a car bomb at a Darazinda checkpoint killed 14 more.

The state's answer is called Azm-e-Istehkam, which means a firm will for stability. Leaders approved it on 22 June 2024 under the National Action Plan, Pakistan's nationwide plan against terrorism. It joins military action with better laws, development and a shared national message.

Security forces now carry out about 194 intelligence-based operations every day. These are raids based on secret information. On 31 July 2026, the military's media wing, ISPR, said 40,348 operations that year had killed 2,084 terrorists.

The price is high. ISPR said 819 Pakistanis were martyred in the same period. They included 303 soldiers, 194 police and other security staff, and 322 civilians.

In August 2025, the National Assembly voted to bring back a power to hold terror suspects for up to 3 months while officials investigate. In August 2026, Punjab's assembly approved secret terror trials to protect judges and witnesses, though critics worry about fair trials.

On 2 October 2026, top army commanders said operations alone are not enough. They called for better local government, stronger police and action against links between terrorists and criminals.

Leaders in Islamabad and Peshawar still argue in public about the right policy. But terrorism should never become a party fight. Soldiers, police and citizens need a single plan and full support.

The people of KP are not the problem. They are the front line, and they have earned peace.
