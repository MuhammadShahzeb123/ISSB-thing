Trump and Xi calm trade, but not the seas.

On 25 September 2026, US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping arrived together at the National Archives in Washington. It was the last day of Xi's first state visit to the US since 2015.

In 2025, the world's 2 biggest economies came close to cutting their trade ties. In April 2025, US tariffs on Chinese goods hit 145 percent, and China hit back hard. In October 2025, the 2 leaders agreed a truce in Busan, South Korea.

In 2026 they met twice. Trump visited Beijing on 14 and 15 May, the first visit by a US president since 2017. The White House said China would buy 200 Boeing planes and more American farm goods.

In Washington, they extended the truce to 10 January 2027. Each side listed about 30 billion dollars of goods for lower tariffs. They also agreed to hold talks on artificial intelligence in November and to open a channel for reporting AI incidents.

Still, the rivalry runs deep, and Taiwan is the hardest issue. China sees the self-ruled island as its own territory. In Beijing in May, Xi warned that mishandling Taiwan could bring "clashes and even conflicts".

US officials say their Taiwan policy has not changed. But Trump is holding back a 14 billion dollar arms package for Taiwan. He has called it "a very good negotiating chip".

The pressure around Taiwan has changed shape. Researchers at the Asia Society found that Chinese military flights near the island fell by about half from January to mid-August 2026, compared with a year before. But sightings of Chinese coast guard and other official ships more than doubled.

The South China Sea is tense too. On 24 September, Chinese coast guard and navy ships blocked a Philippine supply mission to Second Thomas Shoal, where Filipino troops live on a grounded old warship. Manila points to a 2016 international ruling in its favour, which China rejects.

ASEAN, the Southeast Asian group chaired this year by the Philippines, hopes to agree a code of conduct for the sea with China by the end of 2026.

For Pakistan, this is close to home. China is our "all-weather" partner, and 2026 marks 75 years of ties. Pakistan officially supports the one-China principle. Yet the US is Pakistan's most important export market.

A clash between these giants would force hard choices on countries like ours. Calm between them lets Pakistan trade with both and stay out of rival camps.

The mature view is to welcome every handshake, but to look closely at what is traded. Disputes at sea should be settled by law and patient talks, not by blocked boats.

Trump and Xi meet again at the APEC summit of Asia-Pacific economies in Shenzhen on 18 and 19 November. Many in Asia hope the calm lasts longer than the truce.

Points to remember.

Trump visited Beijing on 14 and 15 May 2026. Xi made a state visit to Washington from 23 to 25 September 2026.
The US-China trade truce was extended to 10 January 2027.
Trump is holding back a 14 billion dollar Taiwan arms package.
