Who runs Pakistan in 2026.

Who runs Pakistan in 2026? The answer begins with the Constitution, the highest law of Pakistan.

At the top is President Asif Ali Zardari, the head of state. Members of Parliament and the 4 provincial assemblies elected him. He began his second term on 10 March 2024.

He signs bills into law and acts mostly on the advice of the Prime Minister.

The Prime Minister runs the government. Shehbaz Sharif took this oath on 4 March 2024. He leads the cabinet, his team of federal ministers.

Ishaq Dar is Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister. Muhammad Aurangzeb is Finance Minister and Mohsin Naqvi is Interior Minister. Khawaja Asif is Defence Minister and Attaullah Tarar is Information Minister.

Parliament makes laws and has 2 houses. The National Assembly has 336 members and elects the Prime Minister. Its Speaker is Sardar Ayaz Sadiq.

Parties outside the government are called the opposition. Since 16 January 2026, their leader in the Assembly has been Mahmood Khan Achakzai.

The Senate has 96 members and gives every province an equal voice. Yousuf Raza Gilani is Chairman, and Syedaal Khan Nasar is Deputy Chairman.

Then come the courts. Justice Yahya Afridi became Chief Justice of Pakistan on 26 October 2024.

He was chosen in a new way. In October 2024, Parliament passed the 26th Amendment. An amendment is a change to the Constitution.

Now a committee of 12 lawmakers picks the Chief Justice from the 3 most senior judges. The Chief Justice serves a fixed term of 3 years.

The amendment also lets the Chief Election Commissioner stay until a new one is appointed. So Sikandar Sultan Raja still heads the Election Commission.

In November 2025 came the 27th Amendment. It created a Federal Constitutional Court to decide questions about the Constitution.

Justice Aminuddin Khan became its first Chief Justice on 14 November 2025. The Supreme Court still decides ordinary civil and criminal cases.

Supporters said the change would make justice faster. Critics said it weakened the Supreme Court.

Pakistan has 4 provinces. Each has a Governor appointed by the President. Each also has a Chief Minister, elected by the provincial assembly.

In Punjab, Governor Saleem Haider Khan works with Chief Minister Maryam Nawaz. In Sindh, Governor Nehal Hashmi works with Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah.

In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Faisal Karim Kundi is Governor and Sohail Afridi is Chief Minister. In Balochistan, Jaffar Khan Mandokhail is Governor and Sarfraz Bugti is Chief Minister.

In 2026, Iftikhar Ali Gillani became Prime Minister of Azad Kashmir. Amjad Hussain became Chief Minister of Gilgit-Baltistan.

Last come the armed forces. The President is their supreme commander, and the federal government controls them.

The 27th Amendment also made the army chief the Chief of Defence Forces, the head of all 3 services.

Field Marshal Asim Munir became the first Chief of Defence Forces on 5 December 2025. Field Marshal is the army's highest rank, and he received it on 20 May 2025.

The old post of Chairman Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee ended on 27 November 2025.

Admiral Naveed Ashraf leads the Navy. Air Chief Marshal Zaheer Ahmed Babar Sidhu leads the Air Force.

Lieutenant General Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry is DG ISPR, the head of the military's media wing. Lieutenant General Asim Malik heads the ISI, the main intelligence agency.

So who runs Pakistan? No single person does. Power is shared, and each office checks the others. That balance is what keeps a state strong.

Points to remember.

President Asif Ali Zardari began his second term on 10 March 2024. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif took oath on 4 March 2024.
The 26th Amendment of October 2024 lets a 12-member parliamentary committee pick the Chief Justice for a fixed 3-year term.
Justice Yahya Afridi has been Chief Justice of Pakistan since 26 October 2024.
The 27th Amendment of November 2025 created the Federal Constitutional Court, led by Justice Aminuddin Khan since 14 November 2025.
Field Marshal Asim Munir became the first Chief of Defence Forces on 5 December 2025. The Chairman Joint Chiefs post ended on 27 November 2025.
New in 2026 are Sindh Governor Nehal Hashmi, AJK Prime Minister Iftikhar Ali Gillani and Gilgit-Baltistan Chief Minister Amjad Hussain.
