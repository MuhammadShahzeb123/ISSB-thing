The US-Iran war and Islamabad's bid for peace.

On 7 April 2026, the world was waiting for a deadline. President Donald Trump had threatened to destroy Iran's power plants and bridges. The deadline was hours away.

Less than 2 hours before it, he stepped back. He agreed to a 2-week ceasefire, a pause in fighting. He said he did so after talks with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir of Pakistan.

The road to that night began in June 2025. Israel and Iran fought a 12-day war. On 22 June 2025, the United States bombed Iran's nuclear sites at Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan.

On 28 September 2025, UN sanctions on Iran came back. Britain, France and Germany had used a rule called snapback. Sanctions are penalties that block trade and money.

Talks went on, with Oman in the middle, but ended without a deal. On 28 February 2026, the US and Israel attacked Iran. Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed. On 8 March 2026, his son Mojtaba Khamenei was named the new Supreme Leader.

Iran fired missiles at Israel and at US bases in Gulf countries. It also blocked most shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea passage that carries Gulf oil. Fighting spread to Lebanon.

By 28 August 2026, Al Jazeera counted at least 3,527 people killed in Iran and 4,350 in Lebanon. Iranian attacks had killed 60 Israelis and 18 US troops.

After the April ceasefire, Pakistan hosted the first direct US-Iran talks in decades. US Vice President JD Vance and Iran's Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf talked for 21 hours in Islamabad. The talks ended on 12 April 2026 with no deal.

On 17 June 2026, Trump and Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian signed the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding. That is a written agreement on the main points. Pakistan signed too, as the mediator, or go-between.

Its 14 points promised an end to fighting on all fronts, including Lebanon. Iran would let ships pass Hormuz with no charge for 60 days. The US would end its naval blockade of Iran's ports, and a final deal was due within 60 days.

It lasted barely a month. In early July 2026, a Qatari gas tanker and a Saudi oil tanker were hit near Hormuz. Qatar blamed Iran.

The US struck back, and Trump said the ceasefire was over. On 14 July 2026, the US restarted its blockade.

Pakistan has kept trying. Field Marshal Munir and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi visited Tehran in August 2026. In New York in September 2026, Qatar carried messages between the 2 sides. Trump rejected Iran's 7-day plan.

On 4 October 2026, Iran said Hormuz will stay shut until its 7 conditions are met. It says they are based on the Islamabad deal. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said there is no military solution. The US is also sending a third aircraft carrier to the region.

Why does this matter to Pakistan? Iran is our neighbour, and America and the Gulf states are our partners. A wider war would hurt our fuel, our trade and our workers abroad.

The UN chief has thanked Pakistan for its mediation. But a trusted go-between needs patience, quiet work and fairness to both sides.

Bombs have not given either side what it wants. Talks still can. Pakistan's best tool is its word, kept honestly.
