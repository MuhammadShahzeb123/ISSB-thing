Pakistan and China mark 75 years together.

On 25 May 2026, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif met President Xi Jinping in Beijing. Both countries were marking 75 years of diplomatic ties. The leaders agreed to turn the anniversary into a new phase of practical cooperation.

Pakistan and China set up diplomatic relations on 21 May 1951. Pakistan was the first Muslim country to do so. Both governments call their bond an "all-weather" partnership, a friendship for good times and bad.

That bond was tested in May 2025. During the short war with India, Pakistan flew Chinese-made J-10C fighter jets. US officials told Reuters that a J-10 shot down at least 2 Indian aircraft on 7 May 2025. On 20 May 2025, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi said China would "firmly support" Pakistan in defending its independence and borders.

Defence links kept growing. On 30 April 2026, the Pakistan Navy formally took PNS Hangor into service in China. It is the first of 8 new submarines and reached Karachi on 11 June 2026. In May 2026, the air force said it had an "initial collaborative mechanism" for China's J-35 stealth fighter, a jet designed to avoid radar.

The main economic project is the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, or CPEC. It is a big programme of Chinese-backed roads, power plants and the port of Gwadar. In September 2025, both sides launched CPEC Phase 2. It focuses on growth, livelihoods, innovation, green energy and links with the region.

Money matters too. China keeps 4 billion dollars in cash deposits at the State Bank of Pakistan. In May 2026, Pakistan also sold its first Panda bond, a loan from investors in Chinese yuan. It raised about 250 million dollars at 2.5 percent interest, and buyers wanted more than 5 times that amount.

Some plans move slowly. The ML-1 railway from Karachi to Peshawar was planned under CPEC long ago, but years of talks did not produce a Chinese loan for the whole line. Now the Asian Development Bank, a regional lender, is leading the funding for the first section. It runs 480 kilometres from Karachi to Rohri and will cost about 2.5 billion dollars.

Security is the hardest test. In December 2024, officials said 20 Chinese citizens had been killed in attacks in Pakistan since 2021. In early 2026, Pakistan announced a special protection unit for Chinese nationals. On 31 January 2026, the Balochistan Liberation Army attacked towns across Balochistan, including Quetta and Gwadar.

Why does this matter? CPEC's first phase built roads and power plants. Phase 2 promises factories and jobs. China also says the Kashmir dispute should be settled peacefully under the UN Charter, UN resolutions and bilateral agreements. On 26 May 2026, it even praised Pakistan's role in the US and Iran ceasefire.

The sensible view is simple. China is a steady friend, but friendship needs delivery from Pakistan too. That means safe sites for Chinese engineers, bills paid on time and projects finished. China's flexibility on ML-1 also shows Pakistan can work with many partners at once.

A friendship of 75 years is rare. Keeping it strong for the next 75 depends on the work Pakistan does at home.
