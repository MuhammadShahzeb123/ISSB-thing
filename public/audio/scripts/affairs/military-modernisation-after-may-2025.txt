New rockets, submarines and jets after May 2025.

In the North Arabian Sea, a new submarine fired its first missile. PNS Hangor launched an anti-ship cruise missile at a target far away, and hit it. The military announced the success on 27 September 2026.

Admiral Naveed Ashraf, the naval chief, watched the firing. The name Hangor carries history. In 1971, an earlier Hangor became the first submarine since the Second World War to sink a warship.

This test is part of a bigger story. That story begins in May 2025.

In May 2025, Pakistan and India fought a short conflict. Fighter jets, missiles and drones were all used. It ended with a ceasefire on 10 May 2025.

Pakistan calls this period Marka-e-Haq, the Battle of Truth. Its military response was named Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos.

The conflict showed how fast modern war can be. On 7 May 2026, at a press conference, the military highlighted its homegrown weapons. They included cruise missiles, drones, loitering munitions and anti-drone systems.

On 10 May 2026, the 3 service chiefs marked the first anniversary together at General Headquarters in Rawalpindi.

The army moved early. On 13 August 2025, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif announced the Army Rocket Force Command. It handles conventional missiles, which carry normal explosives, not nuclear warheads.

On 30 September 2025, the army fired the Fatah-4 cruise missile to a range of 750 kilometres. A cruise missile flies low, like a small plane without a pilot. ISPR said it flies close to the ground to avoid missile defences.

The new force kept training. It fired the Fateh-II missile on 28 April 2026 and the Fatah-4 again on 14 May 2026.

The navy grew too. On 4 April 2026, it inducted PNS Khaibar, the second of 4 Babur-class corvettes built with Türkiye. A corvette is a small, fast warship.

On 30 April 2026, PNS Hangor was commissioned in Sanya, China. This means it formally joined the navy. It is the first of 8 Hangor-class submarines. Its air-independent propulsion lets it travel farther before coming up to the surface.

The air force shared its plans on 7 May 2026. Air Vice Marshal Tariq Ghazi spoke of more J-10C jets, much upgraded JF-17s and long-range precision weapons.

He also confirmed a first-stage agreement with China about the J-35 stealth fighter. He gave no numbers, prices or delivery dates.

Drones are now central. In February 2026, the air force's Golden Eagle exercise practised manned-unmanned teaming. Pilots worked alongside drones and loitering munitions, which are drones that wait over a target and then strike.

Why does this matter? The aim is deterrence, which means making any attack look too costly to try. The idea is to strike deep without reaching for nuclear weapons.

A mature view also needs care. An expert at Air University told Dawn it was too early to judge how well the new force would work. Many online posts also claim jet numbers and delivery dates that no official has confirmed.

New machines need trained crews, spare parts and steady money. A missile hitting its target on a calm day is impressive. Real strength is a crew that can do it again, under pressure, every single time.

Points to remember.

The Army Rocket Force Command was announced on 13 August 2025.
The Fatah-4 ground-launched cruise missile was tested to 750 kilometres on 30 September 2025.
PNS Hangor, the first of 8 Hangor-class submarines, was commissioned in China on 30 April 2026 and fired its first missile in September 2026.
PNS Khaibar, the second Babur-class corvette built with Türkiye, joined the fleet on 4 April 2026.
On 7 May 2026 the PAF confirmed a first-stage J-35 arrangement with China but gave no numbers or dates.
