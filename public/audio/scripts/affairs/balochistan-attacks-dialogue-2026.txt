Balochistan under attack, and the door left open.

On the morning of 24 May 2026, a shuttle train left Quetta Cantonment. It carried soldiers and their families to meet the Jaffar Express. Many were going home for Eid al-Adha.

Near Chaman Phatak, a suicide bomber drove a car full of explosives into the train. Officials first counted 24 dead. Reuters later reported more than 30 deaths.

A father, a mother, a son and a daughter from the same family died, along with people in nearby homes. The Balochistan Liberation Army, or BLA, said it carried out the attack.

The BLA is a banned armed group that wants to split Balochistan from Pakistan. Since 31 May 2025, the state has called such groups Fitna al-Hindustan, meaning the mischief of India. Pakistan says India backs them, and India denies it.

Balochistan is Pakistan's largest province but has the fewest people. It is rich in minerals and home to the port of Gwadar.

On 11 March 2025, BLA fighters had hijacked the Jaffar Express itself in the Bolan Pass, with about 440 passengers on board. After 36 hours, 354 passengers were rescued, 26 people were killed and all 33 attackers died.

On 31 January 2026, the BLA attacked more than 12 places at once, including Quetta, Gwadar, Mastung and Nushki. Chief Minister Sarfraz Bugti said 31 civilians and 17 security personnel died, and forces killed 145 attackers in 40 hours.

In Gwadar, police said the civilians killed were ethnic Baloch, including women and children. The BLA claims to fight for the Baloch, yet Baloch families were among its victims.

In early July 2026, militants attacked a police post guarding the Mangi Dam in Ziarat, a project to bring water to Quetta. Over 4 days, the military said, 27 police, 11 soldiers and 4 civilians died in attacks across the province. It blamed the TTP, or Pakistani Taliban, and the BLA.

Militants also attack the highways that feed the province. Government data showed 107 freight trucks burned between January and early August 2026, Arab News reported. Driver Allah Gul lost the truck that supported about 40 relatives.

The Center for Research and Security Studies, a research group in Islamabad, says Balochistan was the worst-hit province from July to September 2026. It counted 829 deaths there, more than 3 times the previous 3 months.

The state has hit back hard. The military said 31,080 intelligence-based operations took place in Balochistan by 31 July 2026. On 9 July 2026, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said in Quetta that the war would go on until the last terrorist is gone.

It has also offered a way out. On 31 July 2026, Chief Minister Bugti said the door of dialogue is open, even to groups guilty of bloodshed. Those who give up arms are offered government jobs, education for their children and health care.

The province says its Youth Engagement Plan has projects to create jobs for young people in every district. The army spokesman said the real stakeholders, the people who matter most, are the ordinary people of Balochistan.

Many families also want answers about missing relatives. The army spokesman said a national commission has received 2,933 such cases from Balochistan and closed 2,767 of them.

The BLA does not speak for the Baloch people. Its attacks have killed Baloch civilians, train passengers, drivers and police officers.

Balochistan needs firm security, but it also needs schools, jobs and fair justice. Every Baloch child who gets them is a defeat for the gunmen.
