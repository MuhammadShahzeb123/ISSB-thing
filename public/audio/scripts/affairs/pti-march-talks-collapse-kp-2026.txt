Talks fail as PTI march sets off from KP.

On Sunday 4 October 2026, a large truck left Gandi Chowk in Lakki Marwat. A huge picture of Imran Khan covered its trailer. On top stood Sohail Afridi, the Chief Minister of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, or KP.

About 5,000 supporters followed in buses, cars and trucks, news agencies reported. They plan to reach Khairabad, on the KP and Punjab border, by 8 October. The government says the march will not be allowed into Islamabad.

Imran Khan founded Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf, or PTI. He was Prime Minister from August 2018 until April 2022, when he lost a vote of no confidence. He has been in jail since 5 August 2023.

In January 2025, a court gave him 14 years in the 190 million pound case, also called the Al-Qadir Trust case. In December 2025, he and his wife Bushra Bibi each got 17 more years in the Toshakhana-2 case, about a jewellery set received as a state gift.

Khan denies wrongdoing and says the cases are political. The Islamabad High Court is due to hear the couple's appeals in the 190 million pound case on 5 October 2026.

His health has become a major issue. On 18 August 2026, the Supreme Court ordered that he be moved to Shifa International Hospital within 2 days.

Instead, on the night of 20 August, officials took him to PIMS, a government hospital, saying this was for security reasons. He was back in jail the next morning.

The government says he is medically fit and gets regular family visits. His sons say he is being kept away from family and doctors.

Tension rose again on 20 September. Police in Lahore detained his sister Aleema Khan for 30 days under a public order law. His 2 other sisters were taken soon after.

PTI postponed its march from 27 September, saying authorities had blocked key roads, including Attock Bridge. National Assembly Speaker Ayaz Sadiq then chaired talks at Parliament House on 2 and 3 October. Talks on 3 October ran past midnight and failed.

Each side tells a different story. PTI's Asad Qaiser said the party dropped every demand except a meeting between its leaders and Imran Khan. Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi said KP's government backed away from promises on fighting terrorism.

Naqvi said security should not become a political fight "while the province is at war".

PTI's Salman Akram Raja said his party has "zero tolerance for terrorism". He wants action against terrorists agreed with the province and backed by local people.

Federal ministers now openly talk of governor's rule or an emergency. Either step could put KP's government under federal control for a time.

On 4 October, the office of KP Governor Faisal Karim Kundi promised its "full constitutional role" if the President and Prime Minister found such a step necessary. Afridi said PTI would accept "neither governor's rule nor an emergency".

This matters far beyond party politics. KP has seen a sharp rise in militant attacks. Dozens of soldiers, police and civilians have been killed or injured in recent months.

Ordinary people suffer too when containers block highways. In 2024, a PTI protest turned violent, and both security personnel and civilians were killed.

The calm path is hard but clear. Courts, not crowds or containers, should settle Imran Khan's cases and his medical care. The fight against terrorism needs the centre and the province on the same side.

Naqvi says the talks are over. Rana Sanaullah, the Prime Minister's political adviser, says PTI can return to talks if it cooperates against terrorism. Another round of talks would serve Pakistan far better than another week of blocked roads.

Points to remember.

Imran Khan has been in jail since 5 August 2023. He got 14 years in the 190 million pound case on 17 January 2025.
Imran Khan and Bushra Bibi each got 17 years in the Toshakhana-2 case on 20 December 2025.
Government and opposition talks chaired by Speaker Ayaz Sadiq on 2 and 3 October 2026 failed.
The PTI march began on 4 October 2026 at Gandi Chowk, Lakki Marwat, and is due at Khairabad on 8 October.
An emergency and governor's rule in a province are covered by Articles 232 and 234 of the Constitution.
