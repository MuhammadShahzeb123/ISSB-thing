Lesson one. Answer the question that is set.

Welcome to your essay course. I am really glad you are here. Over sixteen short lessons we will learn how to write a clear, strong essay, one step at a time. Our guide is the book How to Write Essays by Don Shiach, a former examiner. Everything you hear in this course comes from his advice, told in simple words.

Let us start with a surprise. Two students can know the same amount about a subject and still get very different marks. Why? Because, as the book says, it is not just what you know, but how you apply that knowledge that finally counts.

The first and biggest rule is this. Answer the exact question you have been given. Not the question you wish they had asked. Not everything you know about the subject. Just the question on the paper. The book puts it in three words. Relevance is all.

Picture a student who is asked about the origins of the First World War. He knows a lot about the battles, the trenches and the peace treaty, so he writes all of it down. It may be a fine piece of writing, but most of it does not answer the question. The question only asked why the war began. A good student picks only the causes, and leaves the rest out, even when it hurts.

So before you write a single word, slow down. Read the title carefully, twice. Then ask yourself one simple question. What exactly am I being asked to do here?

The examiner sets the task, not you. Give the examiner what they asked for, and you are already ahead of many students in the room.
