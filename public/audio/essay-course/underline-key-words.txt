Lesson two. Underline the key words.

You now know the golden rule. Answer the question that is set. But how do you make sure you really understand the question? The book gives a simple trick. Underline three or four key words in the title before you plan anything.

Let us try it with a question about Shakespeare. Why does Hamlet delay carrying out his revenge for the murder of his father? Now underline four words. Why, Hamlet, delay and revenge.

Each word gives you a job. Why tells you to explain reasons, not to retell the story. Hamlet tells you it is his reasons that matter, not those of another character. Delay tells you the whole essay is about his waiting. And revenge reminds you what he is waiting to do.

Here is a second example. What were the origins of the First World War? Underline origins and First World War. Suddenly your mind is fixed on the events that led up to the war, and nothing else.

A longer question needs a few more words underlined. One media question asks whether the government should step in to stop a few powerful owners from controlling the newspapers, magazines and channels. The book underlines government intervene, different media, owned and controlled, and few media moguls. Every one of those words points to something you must discuss.

Why does this help? Because it focuses your thinking and stops you from showing off everything you know. The book says it plainly. Pick and choose well.

In your ISSB essay you will have very little time, so this trick is gold. It takes ten seconds, and it can save your whole essay.

Now try the Your turn task. You will see a title from the book about lowering the voting age to sixteen. Write down the key words you would underline, and say in a few words what each one tells you to do.
