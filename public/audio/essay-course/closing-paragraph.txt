Lesson ten. End with a strong conclusion.

Your opening makes the first impression. Your closing paragraph makes the last one, just before the examiner decides your mark. So it matters just as much.

What does a closing paragraph do? It rounds off the essay. If the title asked for a judgement, this is where you give it clearly and sum up your reasons. It brings the essay back to the question that was set.

There is a trap here. Do not simply repeat every point word for word. The book asks you to find a fresh and concise way to restate where you have arrived. And just like the opening, the ending must avoid waffle.

Signal that you are concluding. The book suggests phrases such as In conclusion, then. Finally. As I have argued. As I have shown. Therefore. Based on this evidence. And the bulk of the evidence, then, points to.

Here is the book's example about saving the planet from ecological disaster. It begins, In conclusion, then, I would argue that unless all the countries of the world make some sacrifices, the resources we have taken for granted will run out. It says the most powerful nations must lead the way. It says the time for empty talk is past and the time for action has arrived. And it ends with one powerful sentence. It is the human race itself that faces extinction.

That last line is what the book calls a significant final sentence. It is relevant, it sounds final, and it leaves the reader with something to think about. Try to end every essay with a sentence like that.

Compare it with a weak ending, such as, so these were some points about the topic and everyone has their own opinion. It gives no judgement, no summary and no final thought.

Your turn. Write a closing paragraph for the voting age essay. Signal that you are concluding, give your judgement, sum up your reasons in fresh words, and end with a strong final sentence.
