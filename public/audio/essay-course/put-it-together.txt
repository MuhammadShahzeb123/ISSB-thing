Lesson sixteen. Put it all together.

Well done. You have reached the final lesson. Let us gather everything into one simple routine that you can use in the ISSB essay, or anywhere else.

Step one. Read the title twice and underline the key words. Ask what exactly you are being asked to do. Remember, relevance is all.

Step two. Spend a few minutes on a plan. Jot down ideas, keep only what is relevant, and put the rest in order. One main point for each body paragraph.

Step three. Write an opening of four or five sentences. Deal with the topic from the first line, use the key words, avoid waffle, and map out the ground you will cover.

Step four. Write the body. Each paragraph has one main point, opened by a key sentence. Develop it with specific examples and evidence, not vague claims and not a retold story. Close each paragraph with a sentence that sums up or looks ahead. Link your paragraphs with words like however, furthermore and nevertheless.

Step five. If the title asks you to discuss, weigh up both sides, deal with the counter arguments, and make your own view clear.

Step six. Write a closing paragraph. Signal it, give your judgement, sum up in fresh words, and end with a significant final sentence.

Step seven. Keep a few minutes to check. Complete sentences, sensible commas, correct apostrophes and careful spelling.

And above all, remember the book's own summary. Your essay needs a beginning, a middle and an end. In that order.

You are ready. When this lesson ends, press the button to take the full Essay test. Pick a title, plan, write, and let the AI examiner mark your work against these same rules. Then read the feedback, fix one thing, and write again. That is how good writers are made. Good luck.
