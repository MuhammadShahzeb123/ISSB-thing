Pakistan and Afghanistan after a year of fighting.

On 18 September 2026, a suicide bomber struck a mosque inside the police headquarters in Kohat. Other attackers then fought the police for about 21 hours. At least 23 people were killed, most of them police officers, and all 8 attackers died.

A militant group whose leader is believed to be hiding in Afghanistan claimed the attack. Over the next 2 weeks, Pakistani jets struck Afghanistan 3 times. Kabul then accused Pakistan of backing armed men who crossed into Nuristan, a charge Pakistan called baseless.

Pakistan said its strikes on 1 October 2026 killed 22 terrorists from the TTP and the BLA. Both are banned groups that attack Pakistan. But the UN mission in Afghanistan said 10 civilians died, mostly children.

This was the latest round in a year of fighting between 2 Muslim neighbours who share a 2,600 km border.

Pakistan's main complaint is simple. It says TTP fighters, often called the Pakistani Taliban, hide in Afghanistan and cross over to attack. The Taliban rulers in Kabul deny this.

The crisis grew sharply in October 2025. On the night of 7 October, Lieutenant Colonel Junaid Tariq, Major Tayyab Rahat and 9 soldiers were martyred fighting the TTP in Orakzai. On 9 October, blasts shook Kabul, and the Taliban blamed Pakistan.

On the night of 11 October, Taliban forces attacked Pakistani border posts. The army said 23 soldiers were martyred and more than 200 attackers were killed.

Qatar and Türkiye stepped in. On 19 October 2025 in Doha, the 2 defence ministers signed a ceasefire, which is an agreement to stop fighting.

In Istanbul, both sides agreed to build a system to check the ceasefire. But the talks ended on 7 November 2025 without a written deal.

In February 2026, deadly attacks hit Pakistan again, including a bombing that killed 31 worshippers at an Islamabad mosque. Pakistani jets struck targets in Afghanistan, and on 26 February the Taliban attacked border posts.

Pakistan launched Operation Ghazab lil Haq, and its defence minister spoke of "open war". On 16 March 2026, a Pakistani strike hit the Omid drug treatment centre in Kabul. The UN later confirmed at least 269 deaths, but Pakistan said no civilian site was targeted.

Short pauses came for Eid and for talks hosted by China in April 2026. None lasted.

The UN says 499 civilians were killed in Afghanistan between October 2025 and June 2026. Pakistan's army called that report baseless.

Trade has stopped too. Pakistan shut the crossings on 12 October 2025, and Torkham, Chaman and 3 other crossings have carried no trade for a year.

Traders say Pakistani businesses lost over 278 billion rupees in 9 months. In Landi Kotal, Hakeem Khan shut his family's restaurant and let 11 workers go.

In July 2025, Pakistan, Afghanistan and Uzbekistan signed a deal to study a railway linking Central Asia to Pakistani ports. Such plans need calm borders.

On 5 October 2026, both sides sat in the same room at the Moscow Format, a regional meeting on Afghanistan led by Russia. Pakistan's special envoy, Mohammad Sadiq, said more than 4,700 Pakistanis had been killed in 3 years by terrorism coming from Afghanistan. He called Pakistan's strikes precise and defensive.

The Taliban foreign minister, Amir Khan Muttaqi, accused Pakistan's military of trying to unsettle Afghanistan and said problems should be solved through dialogue. The meeting ended without a joint statement because the 2 delegations disagreed over the wording. Russia said it was ready to help mediate if both sides asked.

Pakistan has every right to defend its people and its soldiers. On 2 October 2026, Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said Pakistan wishes Afghans well, but the Taliban must act against the TTP. Pakistan wants written promises that can be checked.

Still, strikes alone have not stopped the attacks. Lasting peace needs a joint system to check promises, care for civilians on both sides, and roads that carry trade again.

A safe border and an open border are not opposites. Pakistan needs both, and so do the Afghan people.

Points to remember.

The Doha ceasefire of 19 October 2025 was signed by Khawaja Asif and Mullah Yaqoob, with Qatar and Türkiye as mediators.
The Istanbul talks ended on 7 November 2025 without a written deal.
Pakistan launched Operation Ghazab lil Haq after the Taliban attacks of 26 February 2026.
The border crossings have been shut since 12 October 2025. Traders estimate losses of over 278 billion rupees in 9 months.
