Pakistan's voice on the UN Security Council.

On 22 July 2025, Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar sat in the president's chair of the UN Security Council. All 15 members voted for a resolution, or formal decision, put forward by Pakistan. Resolution 2788 urges countries to settle disputes peacefully, through talks, mediation or courts.

The Security Council is the most powerful body of the United Nations. It has 5 permanent members with a veto, or power to block, and 10 elected members. In June 2024, Pakistan won a seat for 2025 and 2026 with 182 votes. It was Pakistan's 8th term.

The seat mattered at once. On 5 May 2025, as tension with India rose, Pakistan asked for an emergency closed meeting. Members called for dialogue and diplomacy. Pakistan was also made chair of the committee that runs UN sanctions on the Taliban for 2025.

In July 2025, Pakistan held the Council presidency for 1 month. Besides Resolution 2788, it chaired a meeting on UN work with the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, or OIC. On 14 October 2025, Pakistan also won a seat on the UN Human Rights Council for 2026 to 2028.

Gaza came next. On 17 November 2025, the Council passed Resolution 2803 with 13 votes. China and Russia abstained, meaning they did not vote yes or no. The resolution backed President Trump's Gaza peace plan, a Board of Peace and a foreign security force. Pakistan voted yes to stop the bloodshed and bring in aid. But it said the text lacked a clear path to a Palestinian state.

Pakistan joined the Board of Peace in January 2026. It also drew a clear red line. Its soldiers can join a peacekeeping mission in Gaza, but they will not disarm Hamas.

Then the war between the US and Iran split the Council. On 7 April 2026, Russia and China vetoed a Gulf-backed draft on reopening the Strait of Hormuz. On 17 September 2026, they vetoed a US draft on monitoring UN sanctions on Iran. Pakistan abstained both times, because it was mediating between Washington and Tehran.

The UN noticed this work. On 21 September 2026, the spokesman for Secretary-General António Guterres praised Pakistan's peace efforts. He said "Pakistan's role has been critical".

On 25 September 2026, Shehbaz Sharif addressed the General Assembly, where all 193 UN members meet. He said any attempt to stop or divert Pakistan's share of river water "will be treated as an act of war". He raised Kashmir and Palestine, and called for Council reform under the motto "Reform for all, privilege for none".

Why does this matter? A Council seat gives a voice in decisions on war, peace and sanctions. Pakistan used it to push dialogue, speak up for Palestine and Kashmir, and explain its own security worries. Before its term ends on 31 December 2026, it will also help pick the next UN Secretary-General.

The sensible view is balanced. An elected seat gives a voice, not a veto. Its real value comes from being consistent and trusted by all sides. Pakistan's careful votes on Iran showed that.

The seat ends in December. The trust Pakistan earned can last much longer.

Points to remember.

Pakistan won its 8th Security Council term, for 2025 and 2026, with 182 votes on 6 June 2024.
Resolution 2788 on peaceful settlement of disputes passed 15 to 0 on 22 July 2025, under Pakistan's presidency.
Pakistan voted for Resolution 2803 on Gaza on 17 November 2025.
Pakistan won a UN Human Rights Council seat for 2026 to 2028 on 14 October 2025.
Pakistan's Security Council term ends on 31 December 2026.
