The secret race to lead the United Nations.

On 29 September 2026, the 15 members of the UN Security Council met behind closed doors in New York. Each ambassador received 1 ballot paper for each candidate. They could mark "encouraged", "discouraged" or "no opinion". Afterwards, the papers were shredded.

This was the 4th "straw poll", an informal test vote. It is part of the search for the next UN Secretary-General, the world's top diplomat.

The current chief, António Guterres, is a former prime minister of Portugal. His 2nd term of 5 years ends on 31 December 2026.

The rules are simple but tough. The Security Council recommends 1 name, and the General Assembly of 193 members appoints that person. A winner needs at least 9 of the 15 Council votes. Any of the 5 permanent members, the US, China, Russia, Britain and France, can block a name with a veto.

By an unwritten custom of rotating between regions, many expect a Latin American this time. In its 81 years, the UN has never been led by a woman.

There are now 7 candidates. Rebeca Grynspan of Costa Rica has topped 3 of the 4 polls. She leads UNCTAD, the UN body for trade and development.

In the latest poll, diplomats said Grynspan won 10 "encouraged" votes. Guyana's UN ambassador Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett and Argentina's Rafael Grossi, head of the UN nuclear agency, got 8 "encouraged" votes each.

Nobody yet knows if any "discouraged" vote came from a veto power. The next poll is expected in October.

The winner will inherit a weaker UN. On 22 September, in his last speech to world leaders as Secretary-General, Guterres warned that "cracks have become canyons". He fears a world split into 2 rival blocs. Money is short too, and the UN cut about 21 percent of its staff posts for 2026.

Pakistan has a direct role. It sits on the Security Council for 2025 and 2026, so its ambassador votes in these polls. The new General Assembly session is chaired by Khalilur Rahman of Bangladesh.

Why should ordinary Pakistanis care? A Secretary-General can warn the world about wars, hunger and climate disasters. He or she can also help peace talks when big powers are stuck. For middle countries, a fair UN protects weaker states from the rule of force.

The mature view is realistic. The Secretary-General has no army and depends on the big powers. Still, a respected and independent leader can keep talks alive when governments stop talking.

The 5 permanent members hold the veto. The other 188 members hold the hope that the winner will serve all of them.

Points to remember.

António Guterres's term ends on 31 December 2026, and his successor takes office on 1 January 2027.
In the 4th straw poll on 29 September 2026, Rebeca Grynspan led with 10 encouraged votes.
A winner needs 9 of 15 Council votes and no veto from the US, China, Russia, Britain or France.
Pakistan sits on the Security Council for 2025 and 2026 and votes in the polls.
Khalilur Rahman of Bangladesh is President of the 81st UN General Assembly.
