Why Pakistan says its terror problem crosses the border.

On 6 February 2026, worshippers filled the Khadija tul Kubra mosque in Tarlai Kalan, on the edge of Islamabad. It was time for Friday prayers at this Shia mosque. Guards stopped a man at the gate, but he opened fire and blew himself up.

At least 31 people were killed and 169 were hurt. Hospital records later raised the toll to 38. It was the deadliest attack in Islamabad since the Marriott Hotel bombing in 2008.

Islamic State, a global extremist group with a long record of attacking Shia Muslims, claimed the bombing. Officials said the bomber was a Pakistani who had recently travelled to Afghanistan.

The capital had been hit before. On 11 November 2025, a suicide bomber killed 12 people outside an Islamabad court.

On 27 June 2026, attackers drove a vehicle into the Karachi headquarters of the Rangers, a security force organised like the army. The attack killed 3 Rangers. Police later said it was planned in Afghanistan and the attackers were trained there.

Pakistan says this is a pattern. On 31 July 2026, the army spokesman said there had been 28 suicide bombings that year, and most bombers were Afghan nationals.

The main group Pakistan worries about is the TTP, or Pakistani Taliban. It is separate from the Afghan Taliban, but they are allies. The state calls the TTP Fitna al-Khawarij, a name that says it twists Islam.

After a deadly attack on police in Kohat on 18 September 2026, the government again asked Kabul to act. It demanded steps against terrorist bases that are real, can be checked and will last.

Kabul denies the charge. On 18 September 2026, Taliban spokesman Zabihullah Mujahid said Afghanistan does not let the TTP use its soil. He said Pakistan's unrest is older than Taliban rule and asked Pakistan to share evidence.

A team of United Nations experts sees it differently. Its report, issued on 10 August 2026, said the Taliban continued their support to the TTP. It also said the Taliban moved about 2,000 TTP fighters and their families away from the border.

The report added that Taliban leader Hibatullah Akhundzada warned the TTP to stop attacking Pakistan or risk losing Taliban support. This suggests Kabul has real influence over the group.

After border clashes, Pakistan launched a military campaign called Operation Ghazab lil Haq against Taliban positions on 26 February 2026. Pauses arranged by Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Türkiye and China did not last. On 21 September 2026, after Kohat, Pakistani jets struck targets in eastern Afghanistan.

Inside Pakistan, the effects are wide. Attacks have reached the capital and Karachi, not only border districts. Pakistan is also sending many Afghans back home.

Diplomacy has not stopped. On 27 August 2026, Pakistan's Foreign Office said talks are possible if Kabul takes steps against terrorists that can be checked. Both sides are due at regional talks in Moscow on 5 October 2026, but no direct meeting is planned.

Pakistan's demand is fair. No country should shelter groups that kill its neighbour's people. The answer must be smart, with a strong border, good intelligence and patient diplomacy.

It also means never blaming all Afghans or all Pashtuns for the crimes of a few. The bombers want hatred, and a united Pakistan denies them that.
