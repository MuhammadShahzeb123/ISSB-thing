Iran, a brotherly neighbour in a time of war.

On 28 February 2026, the United States and Israel launched a war on Iran. Within hours, Pakistan's Foreign Office condemned the attacks on Iran.

It also condemned Iran's attacks on Saudi Arabia and 5 other Arab states. A Pakistani citizen died in an attack on the UAE. Holding both positions at once is the story of Pakistan and Iran in 2026.

Iran is Pakistan's brotherly Muslim neighbour, sharing a border of about 900 km. In June 2025, Israel and then the US bombed Iran. Pakistan condemned those attacks too and said Iran had the right to defend itself.

In August 2025, President Masoud Pezeshkian visited Islamabad. The 2 sides set a target to raise trade from 3 billion to 10 billion dollars.

The 2026 war hit Pakistan quickly. Iran blocked most ships in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea route that usually carries about 20 percent of the world's oil and gas.

Petrol and diesel prices jumped. On 9 March 2026, the Prime Minister announced 2 weeks of school closures and a 4-day week for government offices to save fuel.

Pakistani students in Iran came home by road through Taftan. Iran later agreed to let 20 more Pakistani ships pass through Hormuz.

Pakistan also became the go-between. It helped secure a 2-week ceasefire on 8 April 2026 and hosted US and Iranian leaders for talks in Islamabad. On 17 June 2026, the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding was signed, but it expired on 17 August 2026.

Field Marshal Asim Munir flew to Tehran on 24 August, his 3rd visit to Iran that year. On 4 October 2026, Iran said Hormuz will stay closed until its 7 conditions, based on the Islamabad deal, are met.

Trade found new roads. In April 2026, Pakistan opened 6 land routes for goods to Iran. In July, the 2 interior ministers agreed to open a new crossing and to fight terrorism and drug smuggling together.

The border still has dangers. Iran reports clashes with Jaish al-Adl, a militant group active along the border. In July, armed men burned fuel tankers on the Quetta-Taftan highway.

The gas pipeline from Iran is also stuck. Iran has done most of the work on its side, but Pakistan has not built its part, citing US sanctions on Iran. Iran has taken the case to an arbitration court in Paris, which settles business disputes.

Pakistan also signed a defence pact with Saudi Arabia in 2025. In August 2026, it signed another with Saudi Arabia and Türkiye. Standing with Gulf friends while staying close to Iran is a hard balance.

Yet it is the right balance. A wider war would bring more militants to the border, more refugees and higher fuel bills.

Pakistan needs a calm Iran, a safe Gulf and an open sea. That is why it keeps talking to everyone.
