From Pahalgam to the ceasefire of 10 May 2025.

About half an hour after midnight on 7 May 2025, 4 Indian missiles hit a large group of buildings in Muridke, in Punjab. A mosque and an office building fell. 3 people were buried under the fallen walls.

India called its attack Operation Sindoor. It said it hit 9 "terrorist" sites in Pakistan and Azad Kashmir. Pakistan said the places hit were civilian, including mosques.

The crisis had started 15 days earlier. On 22 April 2025, gunmen attacked tourists in the Baisaran meadow near Pahalgam, in Indian Illegally Occupied Jammu and Kashmir. They killed 26 people.

India blamed Pakistan. Pakistan condemned the killings and denied any role. On 26 April 2025, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif offered to join any "neutral, transparent and credible investigation". That means a fair check of the facts that both sides could trust. India did not take up the offer.

On the night of the strikes, Pakistan said its air force shot down 5 Indian jets, including 3 French-made Rafales. It later said 6. On 8 May 2025, 2 US officials told Reuters that a Chinese-made Pakistani J-10 had downed at least 2 Indian aircraft.

On 31 May 2025, India's top military officer, General Anil Chauhan, admitted there were "losses" on 7 May. He gave no number.

Drones and missiles kept flying for 3 more days. Early on 10 May 2025, Pakistan said India fired missiles at 3 of its air bases, including Nur Khan in Rawalpindi.

Pakistan then launched Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos. The name comes from the Quran, and Pakistan's state radio translated it as Iron Wall. Pakistan's military said it struck 26 targets, including air bases at Udhampur, Pathankot and Adampur. Indian officials admitted "limited damage" at some bases.

That afternoon, the 2 sides agreed to a ceasefire, which means a stop to the fighting. India said Pakistan's director general of military operations made the call. Pakistan's military spokesman said Pakistan never asked for a ceasefire. He said Pakistan hit back first, then accepted an earlier Indian request after other countries stepped in.

US President Donald Trump announced the deal first. He said it came "after a long night of talks mediated by the United States". Pakistan thanked him and later recommended him for the Nobel Peace Prize. India denies that the US mediated. In June 2025, Prime Minister Narendra Modi told Trump that India "will never accept mediation".

In August 2025, India's air force chief said India shot down 5 Pakistani fighters and 1 large aircraft. Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Asif said not a single Pakistani aircraft was hit. In May 2026, the Pakistan Air Force said it had downed 8 Indian aircraft, including 4 Rafales.

Both sides paid a human price. Pakistan says 40 of its civilians died, including 15 children, along with 11 soldiers and airmen. Indian officials said at least 21 civilians died on their side of Kashmir, most of them in Poonch.

Pakistan calls the whole conflict Marka-e-Haq, the battle of truth. On 10 May 2026, it marked the first anniversary with ceremonies at GHQ in Rawalpindi and in Islamabad.

For many Pakistanis, those days showed armed forces that fought with skill and calm. But Pakistan's own military spokesman also called war between 2 nuclear powers "an absurdity", something that makes no sense.

Pakistan proved it can defend itself. The wiser task now is to make sure it never has to prove it again.

Points to remember.

On 22 April 2025 gunmen killed 26 people near Pahalgam. Pakistan denied any role and offered a neutral investigation on 26 April 2025.
India launched Operation Sindoor on 7 May 2025. Pakistan says 40 civilians and 11 soldiers and airmen were martyred.
Pakistan launched Operation Bunyan-um-Marsoos on 10 May 2025. The ceasefire began the same day.
Pakistan said in 2025 that it shot down 6 Indian jets. In May 2026 the PAF said 8 aircraft, including 4 Rafales.
Marka-e-Haq, the battle of truth, is Pakistan's name for the conflict from 22 April to 10 May 2025.
