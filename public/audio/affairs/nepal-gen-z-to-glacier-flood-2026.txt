Nepal's Gen Z uprising, a young leader, a flood.

On 8 September 2025, thousands of young people marched toward Nepal's parliament in Kathmandu. Some still wore school and college uniforms. By the end of the day, at least 19 people were dead after police opened fire.

It started with a ban. On 4 September 2025, the government had blocked 26 social media platforms, including Facebook, WhatsApp and YouTube. It said they had missed a deadline to register.

But the anger was older. Young Nepalis were tired of corruption, a lack of jobs and the same old leaders trading power.

On 9 September 2025, the anger turned to fire. Crowds burned parliament, the Supreme Court and the homes of leaders. Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli resigned that day. In total, 76 people died.

On 12 September 2025, former chief justice Sushila Karki, 73, became Nepal's first woman prime minister. Parliament was dissolved, and an election was set for 5 March 2026.

The vote shocked the old parties. The Rastriya Swatantra Party, founded only in 2022, won 182 of the 275 seats. No single party had won a majority in decades.

Its choice for prime minister was Balendra Shah, a former rapper and Kathmandu mayor. On 27 March 2026, Shah was sworn in at the age of 35, the youngest prime minister in Nepal's history.

Then nature struck. On 26 August 2026, a huge mass of ice and rock broke off Langtang Lirung mountain, near Nepal's border with Tibet.

A flood of mud and water raced down the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli rivers. It swept away villages, bridges and hydropower plants.

A month later, officials had recovered 1,453 bodies. More than 5,000 people were still missing.

Scientists said climate change very likely helped cause the collapse. August temperatures in the area were about 5 degrees Celsius above normal.

Pakistan flew 100 tonnes of tents, blankets and medicines to Kathmandu on 6 September 2026. India and China also sent help.

On 24 September 2026, Shah gave his first speech at the United Nations General Assembly. He said Nepal produces less than 0.1 percent of the gases that heat the planet, yet pays a huge price. He proposed that Nepal, India and China share warnings about dangerous glaciers.

For Pakistan, this flood is a warning close to home. Pakistan's north holds more than 7,000 glaciers, and the 2022 floods killed more than 1,700 people. Shared early warnings in the mountains could save lives in every country downstream.

There is a political lesson too. Banning apps did not calm young people's anger. It made it bigger.

The good news is that power passed to a civilian leader, and Nepal voted again within 6 months. The hard news is that winning votes is easier than running a country through a disaster.

Anger can bring down a government in 2 days. Only patient and honest work can build a better one.
