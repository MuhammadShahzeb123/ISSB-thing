How the 26th and 27th Amendments reshaped Pakistan.

On 16 September 2026, the Attorney General, the government's top lawyer, stood before 3 Supreme Court judges. He read out an order from a different court.

The new Federal Constitutional Court, or FCC, was hearing appeals from 3 Adiala jail prisoners. They wanted private hospital treatment like Imran Khan.

On 15 September, the FCC asked the Supreme Court for the record of Khan's hospital case. It said the cases would be fixed for hearing before the FCC.

Justice Shahid Waheed said the words "fix cases" were "bothering us". The Attorney General called the situation "unprecedented", or never seen before. The bench paused the case for 3 weeks.

The story begins with 2 amendments, or changes, to the 1973 Constitution.

The 26th Amendment became law on 21 October 2024. The Senate passed it with 65 votes. The National Assembly passed it with 225, just above the 224 needed.

Before this, the most senior judge automatically became Chief Justice of Pakistan. Now a committee of 12 lawmakers picks the Chief Justice from the 3 most senior judges, for 3 years. It chose Justice Yahya Afridi, third in seniority, who took oath on 26 October 2024.

The amendment also created constitutional benches, or special groups of judges, to hear constitutional cases.

The 27th Amendment went much further. The Senate passed it on 10 November 2025 with 64 votes.

The National Assembly passed it on 12 November by 234 votes to 4. After small changes, the Senate passed it again, and President Asif Ali Zardari signed it on 13 November.

It created the FCC to hear cases about the meaning of the Constitution, citizens' basic rights and disputes between the centre and the provinces. The Supreme Court now mainly hears civil and criminal appeals.

Justice Aminuddin Khan took oath as the first FCC chief justice on 14 November 2025. The amendment also made clear that no court can question any constitutional amendment.

It changed the military's top structure too. The army chief now also serves as Chief of Defence Forces, over the army, navy and air force. The post of Chairman Joint Chiefs of Staff Committee ended on 27 November 2025.

Officers with the highest ranks, such as Field Marshal, keep their rank and uniform for life. They and the President also got lifelong protection from criminal cases.

The government said the FCC completed an unfinished promise of the 2006 Charter of Democracy, signed by Benazir Bhutto and Nawaz Sharif. It said constitutional cases were delaying ordinary civil and criminal cases. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif said the defence changes would help Pakistan keep pace with modern warfare.

Opposition members walked out in protest. Hours after the President signed, Justices Mansoor Ali Shah and Athar Minallah resigned from the Supreme Court. Justice Shah called the amendment "a grave assault on the Constitution".

For ordinary people, the real issue is speed and fairness. The FCC now gives each lawyer 10 minutes to argue, timed by a stopwatch.

Some lawyers welcome this. Others fear it could cut fair hearings short.

Parliament has the right to amend the Constitution, and forces that work closely together can defend the country better. Citizens also need independent judges and clear rules about which court decides what.

In the end, these changes face a simple test. Can an ordinary Pakistani get quick and fair justice?

Points to remember.

The 26th Amendment became law on 21 October 2024. A 12-member parliamentary committee now picks the Chief Justice from the 3 most senior judges for 3 years.
The 27th Amendment passed the National Assembly 234 to 4 on 12 November 2025 and was signed on 13 November 2025.
It created the Federal Constitutional Court. Justice Aminuddin Khan became its first Chief Justice on 14 November 2025.
The Chairman Joint Chiefs post ended on 27 November 2025. The army chief is now also Chief of Defence Forces.
Justices Mansoor Ali Shah and Athar Minallah resigned on 13 November 2025.
