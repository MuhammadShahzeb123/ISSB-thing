India's year of elections, tariffs and China talks.

On 12 September 2026, Chinese President Xi Jinping landed in New Delhi. It was his first visit to India in 7 years.

He came for the BRICS summit. BRICS is a group of big emerging economies, such as Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa. That afternoon, he met Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

India's foreign ministry said the 2 leaders agreed that differences should not become disputes. For neighbours whose soldiers clashed in the Galwan valley in 2020, that was a big change.

Ties had been warming slowly. In August 2025, Modi visited China for the first time in 7 years. Direct flights between the 2 countries restarted on 26 October 2025, after more than 5 years.

Part of the push came from Washington. On 27 August 2025, US tariffs on many Indian goods reached 50 percent. Half of this was a penalty for buying Russian oil.

On 2 February 2026, President Donald Trump announced a deal to cut the rate to 18 percent. He said India had agreed to stop buying Russian oil. Then the US Supreme Court ruled those emergency tariffs illegal, and Washington turned to other laws.

The war involving Iran then hit oil supplies. In July 2026, Russian oil was more than half of India's oil imports. On 18 September 2026, Trump signed a new law that allows tariffs of up to 100 percent on the biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas. India replied that it must protect energy security for its 1.4 billion people.

India has tried to spread its risks. On 27 January 2026, India and the European Union finished talks on a free trade deal, after nearly 2 decades. Official data showed India's economy grew 7.8 percent from April to June 2026.

At home, Modi's BJP had a strong year. On 4 May 2026, it won West Bengal for the first time, ending 15 years of rule by Mamata Banerjee's party.

The Bengal vote also raised questions. Before it, a review of voter lists removed about 9 million names. The Election Commission said it was removing old and duplicate entries, but critics said genuine voters were hurt.

After the win, the new state government began pushing people it called illegal migrants into Bangladesh. The rights group Human Rights Watch said many were forced out without basic legal checks. This has hurt India's ties with Dhaka.

For Pakistan, the lessons are clear. Trade pressure can hit even a very large economy, and India answered by signing deals with many partners at once.

China, Pakistan's close friend, is also building ties with India. Big countries keep many doors open, and Pakistan can do the same.

Calmer ties between Asia's 2 giants are good news for the whole region. India's 2026 shows that friends can tax you and rivals can talk to you. Wise nations prepare for both.
